## Abstract

Language-model agents exchange information across roles and over time. Individually permissible disclosures can jointly reveal an unauthorized relation, while a permissible early action can change the feasibility of later compliant task completion. We study policy learning under this dynamic contextual-integrity constraint. KCI-Align separates immediate state-conditioned disclosure supervision, continuation-based supervision for currently permissible actions, and verifiable correction at genuinely visited downstream failure states. Its proposed continuation evaluator compares future violation risk, compliant task completion, and successful-path cost in that order, preserving uncertainty and abstaining when evidence is insufficient. We implement native task execution, role-restricted observations, delivery-ledger verification, and shared-model updates. Diagnostic experiments show that downstream execution capability affects the availability of continuation preferences. However, the currently confirmed upstream preferences agree with successful-path and terminal-feedback teachers; independent gains from CCE or DPR have not been established. We therefore introduce a supervision-admission protocol that separates coverage, reliability, objective differences, and acquisition cost before further method training. Main comparative results and independently validated natural-norm generalization remain outstanding.

## Method clarification

For a fixed continuation policy pi, the target is the vector of negative violation probability, compliant task-completion probability, and negative expected cost conditional on compliant completion. The evaluator does not equate a feasible witness with optimal value, or optimal value with the value of a finite-capability model. Each estimate identifies the policy checkpoint, starting state, role-visible observation, sampled actions, seed blocks, uncertainty, and execution cost. Unresolved higher-priority differences cannot be overridden by apparent gains on lower-priority dimensions. Successful-path cost is descriptive unless the preceding comparisons meet preregistered equivalence or noninferiority requirements.

Before training, we compare CCE with successful-path Action SFT, terminal scalar means, confidence filtering of the same scalar, and a CI-first point-estimate control on the same branch samples. A new observation is not itself new supervision. Agreement with an existing teacher does not establish a new optimization mechanism; disagreement with a verified success witness does not establish that the witness is erroneous. We report incremental coverage against each teacher and their union, and retain missing evidence as unresolved. The implemented admission route requires confirmed nonredundant supervision, checkpoint-specific conditional execution capability, and complete acquisition and verification costs. Passing this screen permits a separately preregistered method comparison, not an efficacy claim or automatic training.

DPR is restricted to actual failure states and verified alternative actions at the same decision position. A downstream violation does not retrospectively make a permissible upstream action an immediate violation. Immediate, continuation, and repair supervision retain separate provenance and ablations. We do not introduce another joint-history loss or assume that DPO intrinsically improves on equally informative Action SFT.

## Diagnostic evidence and missing main results

With the same fourteen upstream states, original K8 rule, and sampling seeds, replacing the weak shared policy with a full-workflow SFT policy changed accepted preferences from zero to seven. These are observations from different trained policies, not a randomized estimate of the effect of training. All seven accepted action directions agree with the successful-path and terminal-feedback teachers. Consequently, the subsequent same-label Action-CE training arms are a signal-identity negative control rather than a demonstration of CCE-specific gains.

A separate executor coverage intervention increased compliant completion on a TRAIN conditional-continuation panel from 65/73 to 70/73, while legal direct-disclosure retention fell from 5/7 to 3/7. It failed the preregistered capability gate and was not adopted as a reliable reference. These conditional states are not independent tasks or unseen-root generalization evidence.

The v0.7 development replay verified 512 existing native branch continuations without collecting new model samples. The reference-policy branch evidence remains missing, and no qualified training labels were exported. This is an implementation and provenance check, not a new model experiment. Main Results and Ablation remain **TBD** for CCE gains over strong Action SFT, ordinary terminal supervision, and visible-rule planning; DPR's independent benefit and externally validated natural-norm transfer also remain **TBD**.

## Baselines and limitations

We retain visible knowledge closure for immediate disclosure and public-rule planning for upstream choices as strong controls. A simple same-state terminal-mean proxy is an adaptation, not a faithful reproduction of PrivAct. A change from scalar utility to CI-first ranking must be isolated from confidence filtering, sampling allocation, and optimization. Model capability must be measured at relevant conditional continuations and include legitimate disclosure, rather than only initial-task completion or reduced abort rates. Repeated seeds and renamed views do not create independent task roots. Previously used development roots remain development data; DG36 and P211/P212 remain excluded from development. Source selection for external workflows does not establish the validity of derived norms without independent review.
