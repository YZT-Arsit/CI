# What makes the new value difference semantic

## Scope and access assumptions

The Meeting scheduling inputs and Query name/count/city tables, computational
rules and result truth are reused from the pinned source anchors. The external
recipient R, provider tickets, binding chains, confidentiality rule and completion
receipt obligation are NEW SYNTHETIC adapters. They are not claims about the native
PiSAs/MultiAgentPrivacy task requirements or independently validated human norms.

The task asks for the correct computed result and closure of the original issued
ticket. In a mandatory-binding world, closure includes the provider's declared
ticket-to-key binding chain in plaintext at R. In an optional-binding world the
chain may be omitted. Waiting, another compute worker, or opening another ticket
does not erase the already issued ticket or fulfill its closure obligation.
Cancellation or encrypted/zero-knowledge substitutes would be different output
contracts, not evidence of universal real-world impossibility.

The provider binding declarations are authorized inputs to Planner/Worker. They
are NOT background knowledge available to R before delivery. The operative access
boundary is the role-specific observation and R's delivered ledger. Only the
required schema, not the undisclosed binding values, need be known to R initially.
An explicit control delivers those declarations to R first: a previously future
violation then becomes immediate, and cannot be labelled a both-currently-legal
future preference. This distinction is required for the causal interpretation.

## Cost-free argument

Let I=(subject,ticket) be the initial legal delivery; L be the mandatory binding
chain from ticket to key; H=(key,sensitive_value) be previously delivered knowledge
at R. With no delivered L, I alone does not complete the forbidden join.

After I, every successful task trajectory must deliver its required L to R.
If H connects to that key and the derived relation is forbidden, every such
successful trajectory violates CI. A compliant stop is possible, but successful
compliant completion is not. If H does not connect, actual source data may be
legally delivered to Worker, computed and published with L, giving a witnessed
safe completion. Thus the best strict outcome values differ at task success,
(CI=1,success=1) versus (CI=1,success=0), before costs are considered.

This argument does not use a deadline or a count of procedural actions. It also
does not depend on the raw model's output distribution. It is conditional on the
explicit output obligation, closed-world inference rules and access assumptions.

Required destructive controls: removing the mandatory binding, allowing the
derived relation, or keeping H as an undelivered draft must restore equal safe
success. If the actual source evidence is absent, the solver must fail rather
than recover from global truth. The test suite checks these separately.

## Learning contract

Training pairs come from actual transition rollouts, not from a manually assigned
history score. Both first actions must be immediately legal for future labels.
Ties and states with no safe-success witness are not promoted into primary hard
preferences. Optional-binding and permission controls prevent all-withhold or
all-redact policies from being counted as successful task strategies.

Both learners score finite canonical actions with shared parameters across all
three controller roles. Graph input contains typed candidate/current/required
future edges, endpoint markers and explicit permission/task flags; it has no
precomputed risk, closure or future-success value. Entity equality is supplied by
graph construction and is an architectural capability, not learned language.

The token learner receives serialized typed triples, randomized entity tokens,
role/task flags and the candidate action. It receives no adjacency or equality
matrix. Entity embeddings are fixed random codes, and an additional test uses
unused embedding rows. This tests binding/composition over symbols, not pretrained
language understanding. Neither learner is a pretrained generative LLM.

No safety mask, planner fallback, target action or result oracle is available to
the evaluated policy. Every selected action executes in the task environment.
An execution violation remains a violation in the results.
